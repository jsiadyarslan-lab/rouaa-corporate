The Media Problem

Institutional publishing requires defensible claims at speed.
Most newsroom stacks cannot deliver both.

Verification Burden

Every published claim requires source verification.

Each claim in each article must trace to an official source before publication. Manual verification creates editorial bottlenecks, slows output, and forces a trade-off between speed and defensibility. Aggregated feeds and general AI tools cannot prove where their facts come from.

Source Fragmentation

Official sources live across disconnected systems.

Central banks, regulators, statistical agencies, exchanges, and issuers each publish through different channels, formats, and schedules. Newsrooms spend time assembling information from fragmented sources rather than analyzing and publishing it.

Provenance & Attribution

Published claims need a source chain, not just a citation.

Institutional financial publishing requires every published claim to trace back to its official source document, page, and paragraph. When a claim is challenged, the newsroom must reconstruct the source context — not just point to a URL.

Auditability After Publication

Published claims get challenged weeks or months later.

When a published claim is questioned — by readers, regulators, or internal compliance — newsrooms must reconstruct the source, context, and editorial reasoning behind it. Without provenance preserved at publication time, this reconstruction is expensive and sometimes impossible.
