Methodology

How ROUA verifies, scores,
and governs financial intelligence.

The methodology is the evidence and governance foundation made explicit. Intelligence outputs generated through ROUA workflows are produced through defined, documented, and auditable processes. This page documents how ROUA moves from evidence to intelligence — not how the system is built internally (that is Architecture) and not how trust is enforced (that is Trust Framework).

Methodology Scope

Methodology explains how evidence is transformed into governed intelligence.

Source Registry defines where source material enters the system. Trust Framework defines the controls that make the workflow reviewable and auditable. This page defines the transformation path between them.

Source → Document → Fact → Event → Evidence → Governed Reasoning → Intelligence Object → Output
Methodological Principles

Three principles that govern every output.

Provenance Is Structural

Every fact carries its source reference, document location, and provenance metadata (page, paragraph where applicable) at the moment of extraction. Extraction quality is recorded separately as a confidence signal at the time of extraction. Provenance is not added later; it is the property of the data itself.

Governance Precedes Output

Validation rules, confidence signals, and source hierarchy operate before analysis is produced. Governed institutional outputs are released only after passing the controls configured for their workflow.

Auditable By Construction

Governed outputs are designed to be reconstructable through the evidence chain. From a published analysis back through the evidence chain to the original source document — for authorized auditors, reviewers, and institutional oversight teams, on demand.

Source Hierarchy

How sources are tiered.

Not all sources are equal. ROUA assigns each monitored source a tier — and that tier determines how facts extracted from it are scored, displayed, and governed.

Tier
Source Type
Governance Treatment
Tier 1
Official primary sources — central banks, regulators, exchanges, statistical agencies, government bodies
Highest source authority. Facts extracted from Tier 1 sources carry full provenance and may qualify for direct intelligence workflows subject to configured governance rules. Source authority is separate from extraction confidence, which is scored independently.
Tier 2
Official secondary publications — official press releases, official summaries, official statistical bulletins republished by official bodies
High source authority. Facts extracted require provenance to the original Tier 1 source where applicable.
Tier 3
Reputable secondary sources — established financial media, established research houses, established data providers
Contextual source authority. Used for context, discovery, and analytical comparison. Where a Tier 3 source contributes to a decision-relevant fact, configured governance rules can require corroboration from Tier 1 or Tier 2 sources.
Tier 4
Aggregated feeds, scrapers, unverified outlets, social media
Excluded by default. Tier 4 sources are excluded from the evidence chain and are not used as authoritative intelligence inputs.
Confidence Signals

How every fact receives its confidence signals.

Every fact extracted by ROUA carries structured confidence signals — an assessment of evidence quality and workflow eligibility. The signals are composed of four factors, recorded separately from source authority.

Factor 01

Source Tier

Tier 1 sources carry the highest source authority; lower tiers carry progressively less. Tier 4 is excluded. Source authority is separate from extraction confidence — a Tier 1 source with a failed extraction still has high authority but low extraction confidence.

Factor 02

Extraction Confidence

The extraction system's confidence that the fact was correctly identified from the source material — measured at extraction time and stored.

Factor 03

Corroboration

Whether the fact is corroborated by other Tier 1 or Tier 2 sources. Single-source facts carry lower confidence than corroborated facts.

Factor 04

Recency

How recently the source was published. Recency weighting is domain-aware — different fact types decay at different rates.

These confidence signals are published with the fact — not hidden from institutional review. Institutions can apply their own thresholds for what signal levels are acceptable for what decision. ROUA does not publish a single composite score unless the institution configures a documented aggregation formula.

Verification Workflow

From source publication to governed intelligence.

The verification workflow is the path information travels from the moment a source publishes to the moment a governed Intelligence Object is available for intelligence production.

01
Source Detection
ROUA monitoring detects that a registered official source has published new content — press release, statistical release, regulatory filing, or announcement.
↓
02
Document Ingestion
The document is ingested — extracted, classified, normalized, and stored with full provenance metadata (source URL, publication time, jurisdiction, document type).
↓
03
Fact Extraction
Extraction models identify financial facts and events — each fact linked to the document, the page, the paragraph, and stored with extraction confidence.
↓
04
Evidence Linking
Extracted facts are linked to their evidence records — source, document, page, paragraph, and extraction metadata form the provenance chain that distinguishes ROUA facts from unverified data.
↓
05
Governance Validation
Validation rules check source tier, extraction confidence, corroboration, and recency. Facts that fail validation are flagged for review — never published silently.
↓
06
Knowledge Graph & Intelligence Object
Verified facts enter the knowledge representation layer — linked to entities, events, and the broader evidence chain. The knowledge graph is in active development; the governed Intelligence Object is the output of this stage, available for intelligence production.
↓
07
Audit Record Preserved
The full chain — source → document → fact → evidence → governance → graph — is preserved in the audit record. Reconstructable on demand for authorized reviewers and institutional oversight teams.
Governance Rules

What governance means, structurally.

Source Hierarchy Enforcement

Facts cannot be promoted from a lower tier to a higher tier. A fact derived from a Tier 3 source is not treated as equivalent to the same fact supported by a Tier 1 source, regardless of how many times it is repeated.

Evidence Eligibility

Facts that fail configured evidence, provenance, source-authority, or confidence requirements do not enter the governed intelligence workflow. Institutions can set their own thresholds per workflow.

Intelligence Input Constraints

Intelligence generation operates on governed knowledge derived from validated evidence. Unverified data is excluded from governed intelligence outputs unless explicitly handled under a configured workflow state.

Analytical Traceability

Governed intelligence outputs include an auditable analytical trace linking conclusions to supporting facts, evidence, and documented transformation steps.

Versioned Provenance

Provenance records are versioned and preserved. Updates create new versions while maintaining the historical record required for review and reconstruction.

Audit Trail Completeness

Governed workflow transitions are logged. Material transformations and publication states are recorded so authorized reviewers can reconstruct how an output was produced.

Output Types

Not everything ROUA produces is the same kind of thing.

Institutional trust requires distinguishing what is verified, what is detected, what is derived, and what is interpreted. ROUA labels every output with its type — so your institution knows which kind of claim it is relying on.

Type 01
Verified Fact

A fact extracted from an official source, with provenance (document, page, paragraph) and confidence signals. Example: “ECB deposit facility rate = 2.00%.” Verifiable by clicking through to the source.

Type 02
Detected Event

An occurrence identified in the source material — a rate decision, a filing, a policy change. The event is real (it happened); its implications may require interpretation. Example: “Fed held rates unchanged.”

Type 03
Derived Insight

A conclusion reached by combining verified facts and detected events through governed reasoning rules. Carries an auditable analytical trace. Example: “Rate hold + unchanged forward guidance indicates no change in the stated policy path.”

Type 04
Analytical Interpretation

A judgment that goes beyond what the evidence strictly requires — scenario analysis, forward-looking assessment, editorial framing. Labeled as interpretation, not as verified fact. Example: “Rate hold + inflation data suggests a dovish stance continuing.” Institutions decide how to weight it.

Why this matters: A verified fact can be defended on its own. A detected event is real but its meaning is not. A derived insight depends on the reasoning rules that produced it. An analytical interpretation is a judgment call. ROUA labels each output with its type — so your institution does not treat an interpretation as if it were a verified fact.

Methodology Limitations

ROUA methodology improves traceability, evidence quality, and governance of intelligence workflows. It does not replace institutional judgment, investment committees, or regulatory responsibilities.

Methodology Edge Cases

What happens when evidence is not ideal.

A methodology that only shows the ideal case is not auditable. These illustrative edge cases show how ROUA handles conflicting evidence, stale sources, and failed extractions.

Edge Case 01

Conflicting Evidence

Two Tier 1 sources report different values for the same metric (e.g., revised vs. original figures). ROUA preserves both, links each to its source and timestamp, and flags the conflict for review. The governed output references both evidence records — it does not silently pick one.

Edge Case 02

Stale Source

A Tier 1 source was published outside the configured freshness window for its fact type. The fact retains its source authority but its recency signal drops. Depending on configured governance rules, the fact may be flagged for review or excluded from governed outputs until refreshed.

Edge Case 03

Failed Extraction

A Tier 1 document is ingested but extraction confidence falls below the configured threshold (e.g., unstructured PDF, ambiguous table). No fact is produced. The document record is preserved with its extraction attempt logged — the system does not force a fact from a document it could not reliably read.

Why edge cases matter: A methodology that only handles the ideal path is marketing. A methodology that documents how it handles conflict, staleness, and failure is auditable. These three cases are illustrative — production deployments may encounter additional edge cases depending on source coverage and configured governance rules.
