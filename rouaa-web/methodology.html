Methodology

How ROUAA verifies, scores,
and governs financial intelligence.

The methodology is the trust layer made explicit. Every claim ROUAA produces — every fact, every event, every analysis — is the output of a defined, publishable, auditable process. This page documents that process.

Methodological Principles

Three principles that govern every output.

Provenance Is Structural

Every fact carries its source document, page, paragraph, and extraction confidence — at the moment of extraction. Provenance is not added later; it is the property of the data itself.

Governance Precedes Output

Validation rules, confidence scoring, and source hierarchy operate before analysis is produced. No intelligence is published that has not passed through governance.

Audit-Ready By Construction

Every output is reconstructable. From a published analysis back through the evidence chain to the original source document — at any time, on demand, for any auditor.

Source Hierarchy

How sources are tiered.

Not all sources are equal. ROUAA assigns each monitored source a tier — and that tier determines how facts extracted from it are scored, displayed, and governed.

Tier
Source Type
Governance Treatment
Tier 1
Official primary sources — central banks, regulators, exchanges, statistical agencies, government bodies
Highest confidence. Facts extracted from Tier 1 sources carry full provenance and require no further verification before publication.
Tier 2
Derived official sources — official press releases, official summaries, official statistical bulletins republished by official bodies
High confidence. Facts extracted require provenance to the original Tier 1 source where applicable.
Tier 3
Reputable secondary sources — established financial media, established research houses, established data providers
Contextual confidence. Used for context and correlation, never as sole source for a fact that drives a decision.
Tier 4
Aggregated feeds, scrapers, unverified outlets, social media
Excluded by default. Tier 4 sources are not monitored by ROUAA. They do not enter the evidence chain.
Confidence Scoring

How every fact receives a confidence score.

Every fact extracted by ROUAA carries a confidence score — a quantitative expression of how much institutional weight the fact should be given. The score is composed of four factors.

Factor 01

Source Tier

Tier 1 sources contribute maximum confidence; lower tiers contribute progressively less. Tier 4 contributes nothing.

Factor 02

Extraction Confidence

The model's confidence that the fact was correctly extracted from the document — measured at extraction time and stored.

Factor 03

Corroboration

Whether the fact is corroborated by other Tier 1 or Tier 2 sources. Single-source facts carry lower confidence than corroborated facts.

Factor 04

Recency

How recently the source was published. Recency weighting is domain-aware — different fact types decay at different rates.

The composite confidence score is published with the fact — never hidden. Institutions can apply their own thresholds for what confidence level is acceptable for what decision.

Verification Workflow

From source publication to verified fact.

The verification workflow is the path a piece of information travels from the moment a source publishes to the moment a verified fact enters the ROUAA knowledge graph.

01
Source Detection
ROUAA monitoring detects that a registered official source has published new content — press release, statistical release, regulatory filing, or announcement.
↓
02
Document Ingestion
The document is ingested — extracted, classified, normalized, and stored with full provenance metadata (source URL, publication time, jurisdiction, document type).
↓
03
Fact Extraction
Extraction models identify financial facts and events — each fact linked to the document, the page, the paragraph, and stored with extraction confidence.
↓
04
Governance Validation
Validation rules check source tier, extraction confidence, corroboration, and recency. Facts that fail validation are flagged for review — never published silently.
↓
05
Knowledge Graph Insertion
Verified facts enter the knowledge graph — linked to entities, events, and the broader evidence chain. Now available for intelligence production.
↓
06
Audit Trail Sealed
The full chain — source → document → fact → governance → graph — is sealed in the audit trail. Reconstructable on demand for any auditor or committee.
Governance Rules

What governance means, structurally.

Source Hierarchy Enforcement

Facts cannot be promoted from a lower tier to a higher tier. A Tier 3 fact is not equivalent to a Tier 1 fact, regardless of how many times it is repeated.

Confidence Threshold

Facts below a configurable confidence threshold do not enter intelligence production. Institutions can set their own thresholds per workflow.

AI Input Constraints

Governed AI operates only on verified facts in the knowledge graph. Unverified data does not reach the intelligence layer.

Reasoning Traceability

Every AI output carries a reasoning trace — the facts, evidence, and logic the model used. The trace is reconstructable.

Provenance Immutability

Once sealed, provenance records cannot be modified. Updates create new versions; the original remains accessible for audit.

Audit Trail Completeness

Every step — from source detection to intelligence publication — is logged. No silent operations. No undocumented transformations.
