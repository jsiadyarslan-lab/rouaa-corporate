The Editorial Problem

Newsrooms publish at speed.
Evidence rarely survives the deadline.

Financial publishers, news networks, and economic media groups operate under relentless publishing pressure. Yet the intelligence feeding their stories is too often built on aggregation, not verification — and readers, regulators, and counterparties increasingly demand proof.

No Verification

Newsrooms publish without verification.

Stories are written from wire copy, secondary reporting, and social signals — often without access to the original source document. By the time a claim reaches publication, its provenance has been lost in the editorial chain.

Speed vs Accuracy

Speed is bought at accuracy's expense.

Being first demands shortcuts. Verification is sacrificed for headline speed, and corrections follow — eroding reader trust, advertiser confidence, and the credibility that took years to build. The trade-off is treated as inevitable. It is not.

No Evidence Trail

No evidence trail for published claims.

When readers, compliance teams, or counsel ask how a claim was sourced, newsrooms reconstruct it from memory and chat logs. There is no structured evidence record attached to the published article — only the editor's word.
