Source Categories

Six categories of monitored sources.

Each source in the registry belongs to one of six categories — each with its own monitoring pattern, update frequency, and governance treatment.

Category 01

Central Banks

Federal Reserve, ECB, Bank of England, Bank of Japan, PBOC, and 80+ other central bank authorities. Monetary policy decisions, minutes, statements, statistical releases.

Category 02

Regulators

SEC, CFTC, FCA, BaFin, AMF, SFC, MAS, and 90+ other financial regulators. Enforcement actions, rule changes, supervisory communications, public filings.

Category 03

Exchanges

NYSE, NASDAQ, LSE, Eurex, TSE, SGX, and 60+ exchanges. Trading halts, listing announcements, regulatory disclosures, market notices.

Category 04

Statistical Agencies

BLS, BEA, Eurostat, ONS, StatsCan, ABS, and 70+ national statistical offices. Economic indicators, sectoral data, demographic releases.

Category 05

Government Bodies

Treasury departments, finance ministries, executive economic councils, and 60+ sovereign economic authorities. Fiscal policy, sovereign ratings input, budget releases.

Category 06

International Bodies

IMF, World Bank, BIS, OECD, WTO, FATF, and 30+ multilateral institutions. Global economic assessments, cross-border regulatory frameworks, sovereign surveillance.

Monitoring Methodology

How sources are monitored.

ROUAA does not scrape the open web. Each source in the registry is monitored through a defined ingestion pattern — calibrated to the source's publication habits and update frequency.

Direct Ingestion

Where official APIs or structured feeds exist, ROUAA ingests directly. This is the highest-fidelity path — no scraping, no parsing ambiguity, no intermediary.

Document Monitoring

For sources that publish documents (PDFs, press releases), ROUAA monitors publication endpoints — detecting new documents within seconds of publication.

Scheduled Polling

For sources with predictable release schedules (statistical agencies, central bank calendars), ROUAA polls at calibrated intervals aligned to release times.

Source Lifecycle

How sources enter, move, and exit the registry.

01
Candidate Identification
A new source is identified — through institutional request, internal research, or monitoring of the regulatory landscape.
↓
02
Tier Assignment
The source is reviewed and assigned a tier (1–4) based on its institutional standing, publication authority, and reliability.
↓
03
Ingestion Configuration
An ingestion pattern is configured — direct API, document monitoring, or scheduled polling — calibrated to the source's publication habits.
↓
04
Continuous Monitoring
The source enters continuous monitoring. Health, latency, and publication consistency are tracked. Anomalies trigger alerts.
↓
05
Periodic Review
Every source is reviewed periodically. Tier changes, ingestion pattern updates, and deprecation decisions happen here — never silently.
↓
06
Deprecation (When Required)
If a source loses institutional standing, ceases publication, or fails reliability thresholds, it is deprecated. Deprecated sources remain in the audit trail — they are not deleted.
